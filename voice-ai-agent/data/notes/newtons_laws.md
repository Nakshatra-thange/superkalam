# Newton's Laws of Motion

Newton's first law, the law of inertia: an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed, unless an unbalanced force acts on it. Inertia is the tendency of a body to resist a change in its state of rest or motion. The more mass a body has, the more inertia it has.

Newton's second law: the force acting on a body equals its mass multiplied by its acceleration, F = m a. For the same mass, a larger force produces a larger acceleration. The unit of force is the newton. One newton is the force that gives a 1 kilogram mass an acceleration of 1 metre per second squared. Example: to accelerate a 2 kilogram mass at 3 metres per second squared, the force needed is 6 newtons.

Momentum is mass multiplied by velocity, p = m v. The second law can also be stated as: force is the rate of change of momentum.

Newton's third law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. The two forces act on different bodies, so they do not cancel each other. Examples: a rocket pushes hot gases downward and the gases push the rocket upward; when you walk, you push the ground backward and the ground pushes you forward.
